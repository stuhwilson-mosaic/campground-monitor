How to Use Campground Monitor

Set up availability alerts for any campground or permit on recreation.gov and get notified the moment a site opens up.

💡 How it works

Popular campgrounds on recreation.gov fill up fast — often months in advance. But cancellations happen all the time. Campground Monitor watches for those openings by checking the recreation.gov availability API on a schedule you choose.

When a site opens up that matches your dates, you get an instant push notification on your phone (via ntfy) or an email — so you can grab it before anyone else.

🛠 Creating a monitor

Click "+ New Monitor" on the dashboard to open the setup wizard. It walks you through five steps:

  1. Location — Type a park name in the search box to find it directly. You can also browse by state and agency (NPS, USFS, etc.) if you prefer.
  2. Facilities — Select the campgrounds, or the wilderness permit and its trailheads, within that park.
  3. Dates & schedule — Set your dates, name the monitor, and choose how often to check (every 15 seconds to every 30 minutes).
  4. Notifications — Enable push via ntfy and/or email. Your last-used topic and address are filled in automatically. See the ntfy setup section below.
  5. Review — Confirm everything, then Create & Start to begin watching immediately, or Create Stopped to save it and start later.

⚠ One type of thing per monitor

A monitor watches either campgrounds or wilderness permits, never both. They need different dates — a campground needs arrive and depart, a permit needs a single entry date — so a mixed monitor cannot be checked correctly. Once you pick one type, the other is greyed out. Create a second monitor for the other half of your trip.

🏕 Wilderness permits

Permits are booked per trailhead and per entry date, so pick the specific trailheads you would accept. Quota applies only to your entry trailhead on your entry date, which is why nights is optional and does not change what gets found — it is recorded so you know what to book when an alert arrives.

🕑 Polling interval tips

  • 15–30 seconds — Best for catching cancellations on highly competitive sites (e.g. Yosemite, Glacier).
  • 1–5 minutes — Good balance for most campgrounds.
  • 10–30 minutes — Fine for less popular sites or long-range monitoring.

⚙ Managing your monitors

Each monitor card on the dashboard shows its status and recent activity. You can:

  • Start / Stop — Begin or halt the polling cycle.
  • Edit — Change a stopped monitor's dates and how often it checks. Everything else it watches is shown for reference; to change those, clone it instead.
  • Clone — Build a new monitor starting from this one. It walks the full wizard with every field pre-filled, so you can shift the dates or swap a campground and review the rest before creating.
  • Logs — Every request made, with the URL, HTTP status, how long it took, and what was found. History is kept for 30 days and survives restarts.
  • Test Alert — Send a test notification through your configured channels.
  • Delete — Permanently remove a monitor.

Running monitors always sort to the top of the dashboard, and Running only hides the rest when you just want to check on what is active.

When a site becomes available, you'll be notified immediately. If the same site stays available, you'll get a reminder every 15 minutes. If a site disappears and comes back, you'll be re-alerted right away.

🕐 Monitors stop themselves

About a day and a half after your trip window has passed, a monitor stops on its own rather than polling into the past. You will see it as stopped on the dashboard with no action needed.

⭐ Favorites

If you watch the same places repeatedly, save the selection as a favorite. In step 2 of the wizard, once you have picked your facilities, click Save as favorite and give it a name.

Favorites appear at the top of your dashboard. Clicking one opens the wizard with those facilities already chosen, so you go straight to picking dates. They are private to your account.

📴 Setting up ntfy push notifications

ntfy (pronounced "notify") is a free, open-source push notification service. It's the fastest way to get campsite alerts on your phone — notifications arrive in seconds, no email delays.

Quick setup (2 minutes)

  1. Install the ntfy app on your phone: iOS App Store · Google Play
  2. Open the app and tap the + button to subscribe to a topic.
  3. Choose a unique topic name — this is like a private channel. Use something specific to you, like camp-yosemite-yourname. Anyone who knows the topic name can subscribe, so don't use something guessable if you want privacy.
  4. When creating a monitor here, enter the same topic name in the notifications step. The wizard shows a subscribe link you can share with travel companions.
  5. That's it. When a campsite opens up, you'll get an instant push notification with the site details and a link to book it on recreation.gov.

No account needed. ntfy topics are public by default — the topic name itself is the "password". Pick something unique and you're good. You can also subscribe via the web at https://ntfy.sh/your-topic-name.

⭐ Tips for success

  • Watch several campgrounds at once in a single monitor — just not campgrounds and permits together.
  • Cover backup weekends with separate monitors. Clone makes this quick: clone the original and shift the dates.
  • Use the Test Alert button after creating a monitor to confirm notifications are working before you rely on them.
  • Check Logs periodically to make sure the monitor isn't hitting errors (e.g. recreation.gov rate limiting).
  • Seasonal campgrounds (like those on Tioga Road in Yosemite) won't show availability until their opening dates are announced. The monitor handles this gracefully — no errors, just "no availability".
  • When you get an alert, act fast. Popular cancellations get snapped up within minutes.
